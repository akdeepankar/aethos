What the company does

RACL Geartech is a 42-year-old, Noida-headquartered precision auto-component maker. It makes the metal gears, shafts and gearbox internals that sit inside the engines and transmissions of two-wheelers, three-wheelers, passenger cars, commercial trucks, tractors, ATVs and some industrial and defence equipment. Think of it as a specialist machine shop that global vehicle brands trust to cut, heat-treat and finish gears to micron-level precision - a job very few Indian companies can do reliably at scale.

It sits mid-stream in the auto value chain: it buys steel, machines it into finished or sub-assembled gear components and sells to Tier-1 suppliers (ZF, Bosch-type players) and, increasingly, directly to OEMs - BMW, Royal Enfield, TVS, KTM, Kubota/Escorts, Kawasaki, MAN Trucks and BRP Canada.

Roughly two-thirds of revenue is exported, mostly to Europe (~59-69% of sales). A 16-year, asset-backed presence in the region (5 warehouses) is what management repeatedly cites as its biggest moat against new Indian entrants courting European OEMs for the first time.

Why the stock is in focus now

Three things converged in FY26-FY27:

  1. Consolidated revenue crossed the ₹500 Cr milestone in FY26 - a target management set internally back in 2019.
  2. EBITDA margin expanded to an all-time high of ~25%, as a multi-year, debt-funded capex cycle finished and the projects it funded (BMW Titan/Venus, Royal Enfield, ZF steering) started converting into revenue.
  3. The balance sheet was cleaned up - debt/equity nearly halved from 1.30x to 0.63-0.66x after the FY25/26 equity raise was used to repay debt. This is also why promoter holding fell (dilution).

Where the growth is coming from, in one line

RACL spent 4-5 years quietly building relationships and capacity with premium global OEMs (BMW, ZF, Kawasaki, Royal Enfield, KTM) while moving up the value chain in passenger cars (0% of revenue in FY22-23 to 13% in FY26). FY27-FY29 is the window where most of these multi-year projects move from sampling and validation to mass production at the same time.

Guidance check

What management has actually committed to, and how that has held up across the last three calls.

Needed per quarter, Q2-Q4~₹146 CrTo hit ₹565 Cr midpoint
  1. Feb-2026Q3 FY26 call

    FY27 revenue plan first disclosed at ₹565 Cr ± 5%, alongside an FY27 capex budget of ₹77.45 Cr. On the ₹485-500 Cr then expected for FY26, the midpoint implied ~13-17% growth.

  2. Jun-2026Q4 FY26 call

    FY26 standalone revenue closed at ₹500.22 Cr (+17% YoY). The ₹500 Cr milestone came a year later than the 2019 five-year plan, after fire-related disruptions and KTM's 2024 insolvency. FY27 guidance reiterated - on the actual FY26 base that is ~7-19% growth, ~13% at the midpoint.

  3. Aug-2026Q1 FY27 call

    Revenue of ₹127.82 Cr, +18.4% YoY. Asked whether KTM's recovery and the Royal Enfield ramp justify raising guidance, management declined - guidance is reviewed once a year, and the ±5% band is meant to absorb this kind of variance.

Read-through

Guidance has not been raised despite three straight quarters of mid-to-high-teens growth and margin expansion. Either this is genuine caution (management has specifically declined to over-promise on Royal Enfield and BMW ramp speed), or it sets up a positive surprise if Q2-Q4 FY27 run above the usual Q3-weighted seasonality. The ₹565 Cr ± 5% band is the number to track against quarterly prints.

How big can this become - management's long-term marker

On the Jun-2026 call an investor pushed for a ₹1,000 Cr revenue target by 2029. Management effectively adopted it as a shared aspiration and separately said the vision is ₹1,000 Cr in the next three to five years (FY29-FY31), stressing sustainable rather than rushed growth.

There is some track record behind this. The internal 2019 strategy document ('RACL 3.0') correctly called the entry into passenger cars, the reduction in three-wheeler dependence and the R&D tie-up with ARRK (Munich) years in advance, and the company beat the market-cap assumptions in it by a wide margin. A similar roadmap for the ₹1,000 Cr to ₹2,000 Cr phase is being prepared but will not be disclosed until targets mature.

Bottom line on guidance

This is not a company guiding hyper-growth. It is guiding to structural, high-teens compounding (15-20% p.a.) off a higher-margin base, with a management-articulated path to roughly doubling revenue by FY29-31. The FY27 guide of ₹565 Cr ± 5% is the only board-approved number - everything beyond that is directional commentary.

The stock has re-rated hard. Even after adjusting for growth the multiple looks rich against its own history and against larger, better-capitalised peers like Bharat Forge, Uno Minda, Sona BLW and Schaeffler India, several of which trade at similar or lower P/E despite larger scale and deeper OEM relationships.

What the market is already pricing in

  • BMW Titan/Venus hitting mass production on the guided October 2026 timeline.
  • Royal Enfield continuing its ramp toward the 10,000-20,000/month nomination.
  • KTM's recovery holding.
  • Heat-treatment capex delivering its margin benefit roughly on schedule.

Where the surprise potential sits

  1. Management's refusal to raise FY27 guidance despite three quarters of teens-plus growth and a recovering KTM. A Q2-Q3 FY27 run materially above the top of the ₹565 Cr ± 5% band would be a genuine beat.
  2. The second Royal Enfield project and the ZF-China OEM conversation flagged on the Aug-2026 call are not in any guided number.
  3. Aerospace, robotics and defence optionality sits entirely outside consensus - no capital committed, no timeline beyond 1-2 years.
  4. On the downside - working-capital normalisation (inventory days at 373) is a real, under-appreciated risk to free cash flow that the market may be under-pricing while it prices in revenue growth.

Section 05

Key risks to the thesis

  1. Execution risk from simultaneous ramp-ups

    Titan, Venus, Crystal, the ZF truck EPS pilot, Royal Enfield, Kawasaki's 15-part validation and BRP's shift drums all converge on FY27-28 mass production, while the main Gajraula heat-treatment plant is being rebuilt. BMW's own sign-off has already moved from Jul-Sep 2026 to 24-Oct-2026 across the disclosed calls.

  2. Cash conversion and free cash flow

    FCF negative over 3 years (-₹11.6 Cr), 5 years (-₹71.2 Cr) and 7 years (-₹74.0 Cr) despite strong PAT growth. Inventory days 373, receivable days 117, cash conversion cycle 331 days. FY27-28 capex will likely need fresh debt, reversing part of the deleveraging.

  3. Valuation

    At 39.2x TTM P/E and 5.79x PEG versus a sector median of ~28-29x, much of the growth and margin story appears priced in. A small/mid-cap auto ancillary de-rating, or a soft print against the ₹565 Cr ± 5% guide, could compress the multiple meaningfully.

  4. Customer concentration in new wins

    Royal Enfield, BMW and ZF are each large, concentrated relationships. RACL's policy of capping any customer at ~20% of revenue is prudent, but it also means RACL is deliberately throttling the RE and BMW ramp itself.

  5. Commodity, currency and policy

    Management flagged serious raw-material and energy-cost pressure in Aug-2026, partly offset by rupee depreciation. RoDTEP was cut ~50% from FY27, costing ~₹1 Cr/year with no confirmed reversal. US tariff risk is low-impact today (~2-5% of revenue) but unforecastable.

  6. Tax-rate volatility

    Q1 FY27 effective tax rate spiked to ~47% versus a guided full-year 25.62%, due to a one-off deferred-tax impact from the surcharge change and depreciation/gratuity timing differences.
